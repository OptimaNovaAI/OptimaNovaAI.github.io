I need a partner who is my #2, and that #2 is a combination of a machine and a person. I need someone who used to be a #1 who gets it, can step in for me, and is five steps ahead of me.

Machine prepares. Human converses. That is the design principle, and it is yours. Everything in this proposal is built to it.

There was one more number worth holding onto. Given seventeen duties from your own job description and four places to put them, you sent twelve to a machine and not one to another person. Not once. In your read of that role, work is either automated, or it is yours, or it stops existing.

The Shape Of The Work

Build First. Document As We Go.

A six-week study ending in a roadmap would hand you an answer in late September. By then the Expression of Interest responses have landed, the board book has shipped, and your fiscal year has turned. You would be paying to be told what you needed a month ago.

So we invert it. The systems go in first. The role design, the governance line, and the answer to what your second seat actually owns come out of what we learn running them, not out of a study that happens before them.

Your Words, And The Reason For This Sequence

"I don't want to build a whole new structure on a faulty foundation. I don't want to build it on humans and I don't want to build it on machines, but we've never thought about position-wise percentage allocation to different work."

That is the right instinct and it is the reason the build comes first. You cannot allocate work between people and machines until you have watched a machine do some of it.

One more thing shapes the sequence. You told me plainly: as long as you're prioritizing me, I'm fine. Phase One is you. Not the organization, not the team, not the shop. You.

Phase One · The Work

Four Waves, Set By Your Calendar.

Every date below is one you gave me. Nothing here is arbitrary.

Wave One · August 19 to 26 · Before Italy

Running Before You Leave

Six working days. Two systems live and in your hands before you get on a plane, plus the fix that makes the tool you already own stop lying to you.

  • The daily brief. Lands before you open your laptop. The next five to seven days of meetings, what you need to prepare for each, and what you are still owed by someone else. You asked for this by name and said "today was a day where I really needed that support."
  • Email triage, on your rules. Board and leadership surface. Where you are clearly the one who has to respond, a draft is waiting. The listserv and newsletter volume stops arriving at all, with the exceptions you name.
  • The Copilot prompting fix. Ten minutes of work. You have the license. It has your mailbox. It told you three, then six, then fourteen, then finally 49.2 a day with Tuesday heaviest. Only that last answer came from actually reading your mail. The rest was guessing off a sample because of how it was asked.
Why This Wave Is Not Blocked

Nothing in Wave One requires anything your IT team has to approve. It runs on what is already installed on your machine and already licensed to your account. Elio being out of the country does not sit on the critical path, and neither does the Claude conversation. Those unlock the tier after this one.

Wave Two · August 27 to September 8 · While You Are Away

Built In Your Absence

You are in Italy and not thinking about any of it. That is the point. The three years of Asana history you sent become the specification while you are gone.

  • The return report. Waiting when you land on the 8th. What happened, what moved, what needs you first, and what is now late.
  • Governance and fiscal year transition, mapped. Built from your board meeting, orientation, governance committee, leadership transition, and recruitment projects across FY24 through FY27. Vita held the execution. The process is still in the files.
  • The Expression of Interest evaluation instrument. Built against your published criteria and ready before responses arrive.
Wave Three · September 8 to 22 · The Two Deadlines

Through The Gauntlet

The two hard dates in your September, met with systems instead of late nights.

  • Calendar guardrails. Meeting caps that actually hold, right-sizing 60 to 30 and 30 to 15, recovery time protected after late events, and the texture a scheduling link cannot carry.
  • EoI responses scored, September 18. Every response evaluated against your criteria, questions generated for all candidates and for each one individually, assembled so you walk into the room already remembering what each of them said.
  • Board book support, September 22. Template posted, link dropped, deadlines tracked, late contributors nudged without a human doing the nudging. Assembled so a person eyeballs it once before it goes out seven days ahead.
Wave Four · September 22 to October 24 · The Annual Cycle

The Engine Underneath All Of It

The board reminders, the board book workflow, and the annual calendar are not three builds. They are one engine described three ways: rules, deadlines, chasing, assembly.

  • The annual calendar. Board meetings set first, executive committee calculated at the midpoint, committee meetings sequenced far enough ahead that decisions are teed up, no three in a week, spaced around major events. Move one date and everything downstream moves with it. The Excel file stops being the only place conflicts are visible.
  • Board outreach with escalating reminders. RSVPs, annual gifts, surveys. General messages and targeted ones, with live quorum tracking so you can say forty-nine percent have not responded and we do not have a quorum.
  • Fiscal year transition and orientation readiness for October 1 and October 20.
  • The role design memo. What your second seat owns on day one, what the machine hands them, and what they never hand back. Plus the governance line, written plainly enough to give your team and your IT lead.
The Line

What Never Moves.

You named three things you would not hand to a machine even if it did the job well: anything a board member sees first, relationship follow-up, and crisis communication. Anything with your name signed to it.

Everything built here defaults to draft and approve. Nothing sends without you. As trust is earned category by category, we widen it deliberately and write down what changed. You said you had not thought about who catches it when an agent gets something wrong. By the end of Phase One, that question has a written answer.

How We Work

AI does. Humans decide. Judgment stays with you, and accountability for the work product never transfers to a tool. That is the operating standard inside our firm and it is the one we build to inside yours.

What Comes After

Phase Two Is The Organization. Deliberately Unpriced.

You raised the procurement question yourself, and you were right to. Work that reaches across the Foundation may need to go through a competitive process, and I am not going to hand you a number for something you may not be able to award directly.

So Phase Two is described here and priced later, together, once we know which path it takes. The shape of it:

  • The Salesforce question. Twelve years in, an assessment that mapped every work stream, and three years later it is not where you expected. There is real opportunity in that gap.
  • Team enablement. The systems built in Phase One only compound when the people around you can run them.
  • Organizational governance. The policy, the boundaries, and the traffic light your team keeps saying they will write.
  • Role and structure design across the reorganization, informed by ten weeks of watching what machines actually absorbed.
